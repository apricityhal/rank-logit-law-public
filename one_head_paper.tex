\begin{abstract}
A causal mask realizes prefix averaging with zero learned scores.  We measure
the matrix-level resources used by a position-dependent unmasked softmax head
at one fixed sequence length.  For row-centered score rank \(r\), the minimum
centered logit radius at worst-row output error \(n^{-p}\), with fixed \(p>1\)
and fixed \(r\), satisfies
\[
 \log B=\frac{n-1}{r}
 \bigl((p-1)\log n+\log\log n+O_{p,r}(1)\bigr).
\]
The formula resolves both diverging terms in the fixed-rank exponent at every
fixed polynomial target.  The proof expands a determinant for the lower bound and builds a
cost-balanced rank-\(r\) matrix for the upper bound.  A companion estimate
uniform in rank forces \(\Omega(n)\) rank under a polynomial radius budget when
\(p>3/2\).  A factor-norm inequality transfers the radius
requirement to dot-product factors.  In a range-only exact-real model, the
finite float32 factor range excludes the \(p=2\) target for position-indexed
pure dot-product scores at head dimension
\(128\) once \(n\ge4166\).  The same
exact-real, float32-range assumptions require ambient head dimension above
\(252\) at \(n=8192\).  A finite-size audit places the construction between a
row-weighted determinant bound and its closed-form budget.  Every statement
concerns one fixed pure dot-product attention matrix and holds uniformly over
bounded value streams while that matrix stays fixed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

For prefix averaging, a causal mask supplies a moving support boundary while
keeping every visible score flat.  Let
\[
 T(i,j)=\frac1i\mathbf 1[j\le i]
\]
be the prefix-average operator.  A masked head with every visible score equal
to zero realizes \(T\) exactly.  An unmasked softmax assigns positive mass to
every key.  Accurate realization requires nearly equal scores on each prefix
together with strong suppression of every future key.  Low score rank makes
that joint cost accumulate across positions.

For an unmasked score matrix \(S\), let
\(P_0=I_n-\one\one^\top/n\).  The centered score rank is
\(\rho(S)=\rank(SP_0)\) and centered logit radius
\(B(S)=\max_{i,j}|(SP_0)(i,j)|\).  For a dot-product head of dimension
\(d_h\), \(\rho(S)\le d_h\).  Any matrix with \(\rho(S)\le r\) admits an
\(r\)-dimensional factorization because
\(\softmax(S)=\softmax(SP_0)\).  Equal finite row-softmax matrices have logits
that differ by rowwise constants, since their pairwise log-probability ratios
agree.  Thus \(\rho\) is the minimum factor dimension after quotienting row
shifts.  At every fixed \(p>1\) and fixed
\(r\), the main theorem gives
\[
 r\log B(S)
 =(n-1)\bigl((p-1)\log n+\log\log n+O_{p,r}(1)\bigr)
\]
at the optimum for error \(n^{-p}\).  For a position-dependent dot-product head with fixed
dimension \(d_h\), the required
centered score magnitude grows as \(\exp(\Theta(n\log n/d_h))\).  A fixed
polynomial score budget requires dimension growing linearly with sequence
length when \(p>3/2\).  The excluded lengths in Section~\ref{sec:numerical}
sit one to two orders of magnitude below deployed decoder contexts.

The result concerns one fixed, length-specific pure dot-product matrix evaluated
uniformly over bounded value streams.  Section~\ref{sec:scope} states the
remaining model boundaries.

The table compares the realizations at a common accuracy \(\varepsilon\).  Its
third row uses \(\varepsilon=n^{-p}\) for fixed \(p>1\), where the two-term law
applies.  The masked row uses zero learned scores.  Its architectural support
constraint supplies the forbidden entries and falls outside the score charge.

The lower-bound proof takes first differences across all causal boundaries,
producing a square matrix whose strict lower triangle is small, whose diagonal
is large, and whose rank is at most \(r\).  A columnwise determinant expansion
removes every term that uses more than \(r\) columns from this matrix, giving
the factor \((n-1)/r\) that fixed \((r+1)\)-minors miss.  Because the argument
works on principal submatrices, it can omit inaccurate rows.

The upper bound assigns the nonterminal rows to \(r\) consecutive blocks.
Score drops grow geometrically inside each block, with block boundaries chosen
to balance their logarithmic cost.  The lower and upper bounds agree through
\((p-1)\log n\) and \(\log\log n\) in the exponent.

\section{Operational model}

Let \(S(i,j)=\langle q_i,k_j\rangle\) and
\(A=\softmax_{\rm row}(S)\).  We take \(S\) to be the entire pre-softmax
score matrix.  Any additive positional-bias matrix belongs in \(S\) and in
its rank and radius charges.  The conventional
\(1/\sqrt{d_h}\) scale can be absorbed into the factors.  Softmax is invariant
under row shifts, so
\(SP_0\) is the identifiable score matrix.  Its radius controls every
within-row score difference:
\[
 |S(i,j)-S(i,t)|\le 2B(S).
\]
For a bounded value stream \(v\), the output is \(Av\).  The operational
error is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:operator}
 \sup_{\|v\|_\infty\le1}\|(A-T)v\|_\infty
 =\max_i\|A(i,:)-T(i,:)\|_1 .
\end{equation}
H\"older's inequality gives the upper bound, and the sign vector of the
largest-error row gives equality.  The metric asks one realized attention
matrix to average every bounded value stream.

Uniformity ranges over separate value streams while \(A\) stays fixed.  A
single fixed value matrix tests the column span that it presents to the head.

The radius \(B(S)\) also admits an architectural bound.

\begin{proposition}[Factor-norm bound]\label{prop:norms}
Let \(S=QK^\top\) with rows \(q_i,k_j\in\R^{d_h}\), and let
\(\bar k=\frac1n\sum_jk_j\).  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:norms}
 B(S)\le\max_i\|q_i\|_2\cdot\max_j\|k_j-\bar k\|_2 .
\end{equation}
If in addition every entry of \(Q\) and \(K\) has magnitude at most \(F\),
then \(\log B(S)\le\log 2+\log d_h+2\log F\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Row \(i\) of \(SP_0\) has entries
\(\langle q_i,k_j\rangle-\frac1n\sum_t\langle q_i,k_t\rangle
=\langle q_i,k_j-\bar k\rangle\), and Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\eqref{eq:norms}.  Under the entrywise bound,
\(\|q_i\|_2\le\sqrt{d_h}F\) and
\(\|k_j-\bar k\|_2\le2\sqrt{d_h}F\).
\end{proof}

The lower bounds force a product of query and centered-key norm
envelopes.  A finite exponent range places an additive cap on \(\log B(S)\).
Under the conventional
\(1/\sqrt{d_h}\) scaling the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:norms} is divided by
\(\sqrt{d_h}\).

Write \([m]=\{1,\ldots,m\}\).  For a finite set, \(\osc\) denotes the maximum
minus the minimum, and \(\Stirling{m}{k}\) denotes a Stirling number of the
second kind.  Let
\(I\subseteq[n-1]\) and put
\[
 I^+=I\cup\{j+1:j\in I\}.
\]
Row \(i\in I\) has a causal \((\delta_i,\gamma_i)\) profile
\emph{on \(I\)} when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:profile}
 \osc_{j\in I^+,\ j\le i}S(i,j)\le\delta_i,
 \qquad S(i,i+1)\le S(i,i)-\gamma_i.
\end{equation}
Accuracy sets explicit row-dependent parameters.

\begin{lemma}[Output error gives score geometry]\label{lem:profile}
If \(\max_j|A(i,j)-T(i,j)|\le\varepsilon\) and \(i\varepsilon<1/2\), then
row \(i\) satisfies \eqref{eq:profile} on every \(I\), with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rowprofile}
 \delta_i=\log\frac{1+i\varepsilon}{1-i\varepsilon},
 \qquad
 \gamma_i=\log\frac{1-i\varepsilon}{i\varepsilon}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

The hypothesis uses the entrywise matrix error
\(\max_{i,j}|A(i,j)-T(i,j)|\le\varepsilon\).  It follows from the operational
row-\(\ell_1\) metric \(\|A(i,:)-T(i,:)\|_1\le\varepsilon\) in
\eqref{eq:operator}.  Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} constructs a matrix that
attains the row-\(\ell_1\) target.  The norm sandwich
\(\|x\|_\infty\le\|x\|_q\le\|x\|_1\) establishes the same two-term law for
maximum row-\(\ell_q\) error at every fixed \(1\le q\le\infty\).  In probe
language, entrywise matrix error corresponds to value streams with
\(\|v\|_1\le1\).

The scale \(1/n\) has a direct interpretation.  For a binary value stream,
\(i(Tv)_i\) is an integer prefix sum.  Rounding \(i(Av)_i\) recovers that sum
whenever the scalar output error is below \(1/(2i)\).  The eligibility
condition \(i\varepsilon<1/2\) marks this recovery scale.  Fixed targets
\(n^{-p}\), \(p>1\), give every nonterminal boundary a polynomial margin.

\section{The rank--logit law}

The finite inequality below supplies the matrix-theoretic core.

\begin{theorem}[Row-subset determinant bound]\label{thm:subset}
Let \(\varnothing\ne I\subseteq[n-1]\), \(m=|I|\), and suppose the rows in
\(I\) satisfy \eqref{eq:profile} on \(I\), with positive
\(\delta_i,\gamma_i\).  If \(1\le r\),
\(\rho(S)\le r\) and \(B(S)\le B\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted}
 \prod_{i\in I}\gamma_i
 \le
 \sum_{k=1}^{\min\{r,m\}}
 k!\Stirling{m}{k}(2B)^k
 \max_{\substack{R\subseteq I\\|R|=k}}
 \prod_{i\in I\setminus R}\delta_i .
\end{equation}
If additionally \(\delta_i\le\delta\) and
\(\gamma_i\ge\gamma\) on \(I\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constant}
 \gamma^m\le
 \sum_{k=1}^{\min\{r,m\}}
 k!\Stirling{m}{k}\delta^{m-k}(2B)^k .
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The theorem uses the eligible rows in \(I\), meaning rows with
\(i\varepsilon<1/2\).  Accuracy on \(\alpha n\) such rows replaces the
determinant size \(n-1\) by \(\alpha n\), up to rounding; a corresponding
uniform asymptotic law also requires their \(\delta_i,\gamma_i\) to have
uniform margins.  When
\(\varepsilon=o(1/n)\), every nonterminal row is eligible, and average row
error at most \(\varepsilon/2\) supplies at least \(n/2-1\) accurate
nonterminal rows by Markov's inequality, up to rounding.  The determinant
argument uses only this rowwise consequence.

For \(0<\varepsilon<1\) and \(1\le r\le n-1\), define
\[
 B^{\rm out}_{r}(n,\varepsilon)=\inf\left\{B(S):
 \rho(S)\le r,\ 
 \sup_{\|v\|_\infty\le1}\|(A-T)v\|_\infty\le\varepsilon\right\},
 \qquad B^{\rm out}_{r,p}(n)=B^{\rm out}_{r}(n,n^{-p}).
\]
Write \(N=n-1\) through the rest of this section.

\begin{theorem}[Cost-balanced rank-\(r\) construction]\label{thm:upper}
Let \(n\ge2\), \(0<\varepsilon\le1/n\), \(N=n-1\), and
\(1\le r\le N\).  There is \(S\in\R^{n\times n}\) with
\(\rank(S)=\rho(S)=r\), output error at most \(\varepsilon\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:upperbudget}
 \log B(S)\le
 \frac Nr\log\frac{64e\log(8n/\varepsilon)}{n\varepsilon}
 +\log\log\frac{8n}{\varepsilon}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Two-term fixed-rank output law]\label{thm:sharp}
For every fixed \(p>1\) and fixed positive integer \(r\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp}
 \log B^{\rm out}_{r,p}(n)
 =\frac{n-1}{r}
 \bigl((p-1)\log n+\log\log n+O_{p,r}(1)\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set \(\varepsilon=n^{-p}\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), every
nonterminal row is eligible in Lemma~\ref{lem:profile}, and
\[
 \delta_i\le\delta=2n^{1-p}(1+o(1)),\qquad
 \gamma_i\ge\gamma=(p-1)\log n+o(1).
\]
Two entries in a profiled row differ by at least \(\gamma\), so
\(2B\ge\gamma\).  Eventually \(\gamma\ge\delta\) and \(N\ge r\).  Apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:subset} with \(I=[N]\).  The estimates
\(k!\Stirling{N}{k}\le k^N\le r^N\) give
\[
 \gamma^N\le r^{N+1}\delta^{N-r}(2B)^r,
 \qquad
 \log B\ge\frac Nr\log\frac{\gamma}{r\delta}
 +\log\frac{\delta}{2}-\frac1r\log r.
\]
Here
\[
 \log\frac{\gamma}{r\delta}
 =(p-1)\log n+\log\log n+O_{p,r}(1).
\]
The remaining term \(\log(\delta/2)=O_p(\log n)\) fits inside
\((N/r)O_{p,r}(1)\), which proves the required lower estimate.

Theorem~\ref{thm:upper} supplies a score matrix whose output error is at most
\(\varepsilon\) and whose radius obeys
\[
 \log B\le\frac Nr
 \log\frac{64e\log(8n/\varepsilon)}{n\varepsilon}
 +\log\log\frac{8n}{\varepsilon}.
\]
Substitution of \(\varepsilon=n^{-p}\) turns the logarithm multiplied by
\(N/r\) into
\((p-1)\log n+\log\log n+O_p(1)\).  Its final additive term is
\(O_p(\log\log n)\), which also fits inside \((N/r)O_{p,r}(1)\).
\end{proof}

The \(O_{p,r}(1)\) term permits a multiplicative
\(\exp(O(n/r))\) uncertainty in \(B\).
\footnote{For growing \(r\), the permutation count in
\eqref{eq:constant} contributes \(-((n-1)/r)\log r\).
Appendix~\ref{app:hadamard} gives a complementary Hadamard bound that removes
\(\log r\) at a cost of \(\tfrac12\log n\), together with the all-rank floor
\(B\ge\max_i\gamma_i/2\).}

\begin{corollary}[Head-dimension tradeoff]\label{cor:head}
Fix \(p>1\) and \(d_h\).  A position-dependent unmasked pure dot-product
head of dimension
\(d_h\) that reaches error \(n^{-p}\) obeys, for some constant
\(C_{p,d_h}\) and all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 d_h\log B(S)\ge
 (n-1)\bigl((p-1)\log n+\log\log n-C_{p,d_h}\bigr).
\]
\end{corollary}

The fixed-rank remainder depends on \(r\).  After normalization by \(N/r\),
the budget in \eqref{eq:upperbudget} equals
\((p-1)\log n+\log\log n+O_p(1)\) uniformly for
\(r=o(N/\log\log n)\).  The determinant count introduces \(-\log r\) into
the lower estimate.  Both diverging terms continue to match when
\(\log r=o(\log\log n)\); equation~\eqref{eq:sharp} states the asymptotic with
\(r\) fixed.  At full centered rank, Proposition~\ref{prop:fullrank} attains
\(\log B=\log\log(n/\varepsilon)+O(1)\).  At \(r=N\), the closed-form budget
\eqref{eq:upperbudget} gives
\((p-1)\log n+2\log\log n+O_p(1)\), and the singleton-block realization itself
has \(\log B=\log\log n+O_p(1)\).  Appendix~\ref{app:hadamard} develops the
growing-rank estimate.

\begin{corollary}[Polynomial radius budget]\label{cor:budget}
Fix \(p>3/2\) and \(c>0\).  A score matrix with \(B(S)\le n^c\) and output
error at most \(n^{-p}\) has
\[
 \rho(S)\ge
 \left(\frac{p-3/2}{c+p-1}-o(1)\right)n.
\]
\end{corollary}

The present uniform-in-rank Hadamard estimate yields a positive coefficient
for \(p>3/2\).  Behavior at the threshold remains open.

The finite inequality separates the accuracy regimes more precisely than a
single asymptotic formula.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
accuracy \(\varepsilon\) & eligible rows & status for fixed \(r\) \\
\midrule
\(n^{-p}\), fixed \(p>1\) & all nonterminal
 & two-term law, Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp} \\
general \(o(1/n)\) & all nonterminal
 & two bounds with unmatched leading terms \\
\(c/n\), fixed \(c>0\) & linear subset
 & \(\Omega_{c,r}(n/r)\) to \(O_{c,r}(n\log\log n/r)\) \\

 & lower bound; construction open \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

At \(\varepsilon=1/(n\log n)\), the determinant bound gives
\((N/r)(\log\log n+\log\log\log n+O(1))\).  The budget in
\eqref{eq:upperbudget} gives \((N/r)(2\log\log n+O(1))\).  Their leading
coefficients differ, which restricts \eqref{eq:sharp} to polynomial targets.
For \(\varepsilon=c/n\) with fixed \(c>0\), a doubly logarithmic gap remains.
At larger \(\varepsilon\), Lemma~\ref{lem:profile} certifies
\(\Theta(1/\varepsilon)\) rows.  That count records the reach of the current
proof.  The behavior of \(B^{\rm out}_r\) across the \(1/n\) scale remains an
open question.

Full centered rank changes the scaling.

\begin{proposition}[Full-rank two-level scores]\label{prop:fullrank}
For \(\Gamma>0\), set
\[
 S_\Gamma(i,j)=-\Gamma\,\mathbf1[j>i].
\]
Then \(\rank(S_\Gamma P_0)=n-1\), \(B(S_\Gamma)\le\Gamma\), and every
nonterminal row has a causal \((0,\Gamma)\) profile on every \(I\).  Taking
\(\Gamma=\log(2(n-1)/\varepsilon)\), for \(0<\varepsilon\le1\), gives
output error at most \(\varepsilon\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The submatrix on rows \(1,\ldots,n-1\) and columns \(2,\ldots,n\) is
upper triangular with diagonal \(-\Gamma\), so \(\rank(S_\Gamma)\ge n-1\).
Column \(1\) of \(S_\Gamma\) is zero, since \(1>i\) fails for every
\(i\ge1\), so \(e_1\in\ker S_\Gamma\), the rank is exactly \(n-1\), and
\(\ker S_\Gamma=\operatorname{span}\{e_1\}\).  If
\(S_\Gamma P_0x=0\), then \(P_0x\) lies in this kernel and has coordinate
sum zero, which gives \(P_0x=0\) and
\(\ker(S_\Gamma P_0)=\operatorname{span}\{\one\}\).  The centered rank is
\(n-1\).  Row centering keeps every
entry within the raw row range \(\Gamma\).  The future leakage in every row
is at most \((n-1)e^{-\Gamma}\le\varepsilon/2\); the prefix conditional
distribution is uniform, so the row-\(\ell_1\) error is at most
\(\varepsilon\).
\end{proof}

The matrix \(S_\Gamma\) gives a finite-\(\Gamma\) approximation to the causal
mask.  It uses full centered rank and a growing realized radius to approximate
the architectural support boundary.

\section{Determinant mechanism and matching construction}\label{sec:mechanism}

Let \(N=n-1\), let \(\Delta\in\R^{n\times N}\) be the first-difference
operator, and take the first \(N\) rows of \(S\Delta\):
\[
 D(i,j)=S(i,j+1)-S(i,j).
\]
Since \(P_0\Delta=\Delta\), \(\rank(D)\le\rho(S)\).  Row \(i\)'s profile
gives a small strict lower triangle and a large diagonal:
\[
 |D(i,j)|\le\delta_i\quad(j<i),\qquad
 D(i,i)\le-\gamma_i.
\]
Let \(U\) be the upper-triangular part of \(D\) and \(E=D-U\).  Then
\(|\det U|\ge\prod_i\gamma_i\).  Expand \(\det(D-E)\) by columns.  A term
using more than \(r\) columns of \(D\) vanishes by rank.  Summed over all
\(k\)-subsets \(K\) of columns drawn from \(D\), the number of surviving pairs
\((K,\pi)\) is \(k!\Stirling{N}{k}\); the count for an individual \(K\) is at
most this and depends on \(K\).  Each term uses \(k\) entries bounded by \(2B\) and
\(N-k\) lower-triangular entries.  Keeping their row indices yields
\eqref{eq:weighted}.  Taking a principal submatrix \(D[I,I]\) proves the
row-subset form.  Appendix~\ref{app:determinant} supplies the complete
counting argument.

The construction balances row budgets over \(r\) consecutive blocks.  One
outer product creates the score drops inside each block.  Appendix~\ref{app:construction}
gives the factorization and verifies its centered rank,
row error, and radius bound \eqref{eq:upperbudget}.

\section{Numerical checks and factor range}\label{sec:numerical}

The audit evaluates the explicit construction and the finite determinant
inequality at \(p=2\).  High-precision decimal arithmetic computes the
row-\(\ell_1\) error and centered radius.  The lower curve solves
\eqref{eq:weighted} with the row profile from Lemma~\ref{lem:profile} and the
all-rank floor.

Every quantity in the finite inequality is measurable on a realized score
matrix: per-row oscillations and drops, the centered radius, and the factor
dimension can be read off logits directly, so \eqref{eq:weighted} evaluates
at any \((n,r,\varepsilon)\) with no asymptotic step.  The test is
one-directional: violation excludes accuracy \(\varepsilon\) for the measured
resources; satisfaction certifies nothing.

For a radius value \(B\), define its normalized remainder at \(p=2\), the
value used throughout this section, by
\[
 R_{n,r}(B)=\frac{r}{n-1}\log B-\bigl(\log n+\log\log n\bigr),
\]
the general leading term being \((p-1)\log n+\log\log n\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp} predicts \(R_{n,r}=O_r(1)\) for fixed \(r\).
Figure~\ref{fig:bracket} places the construction between the finite lower bound
and its closed-form budget in all 36 records.  Every measured row error is
below \(0.251\,n^{-2}\).  The displayed interval bounds the unknown optimum.

\subsection{A representability corollary}

Proposition~\ref{prop:norms} converts the finite lower bound into a statement
about every exact-real head with bounded factor entries.

\begin{corollary}[Representable factors]\label{cor:precision}
Fix \(p>1\) and a floating-point format whose largest finite magnitude is
\(F\).  Let \(Q,K\in\R^{n\times d_h}\) have entries bounded in magnitude by
\(F\), and evaluate \(QK^\top\) and softmax over the exact reals.  Worst-row
error at most \(n^{-p}\) requires the finite inequality
\eqref{eq:weighted}, with \(r=d_h\), to admit
\(\log B\le\log 2+\log d_h+2\log F\).  Failure of this condition excludes
such a head.
\end{corollary}

The floating-point format supplies a dynamic-range bound on exact-real factors.
Evaluation of \eqref{eq:weighted} at \(p=2\) gives the table.  Each entry is
the largest \(n\) admitted by the finite bound.  The bfloat16 and float32
thresholds are computed from distinct exact maxima (\(\log F=88.7189\) and
\(88.7228\)); both round to the displayed value, and the admitted lengths
coincide.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
format & \(\log F\) & \(d_h=8\) & \(32\) & \(64\) & \(128\) \\
\midrule
float16          & \(11.09\) & \(65\)  & \(262\)  & \(531\)  & \(1075\) \\
bfloat16         & \(88.72\) & \(268\) & \(1053\) & \(2093\) & \(4165\) \\
float32          & \(88.72\) & \(268\) & \(1053\) & \(2093\) & \(4165\) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The first failing length remains excluded as \(n\) grows because each
\(\delta_i\) decreases and each \(\gamma_i\) increases on the same eligible
row subset.  At \(n=8192\), the finite inequality gives \(\log B\ge418.72\)
for \(r=128\).  Proposition~\ref{prop:norms} then forces
\[
 \max_i\|q_i\|_2\max_j\|k_j-\bar k\|_2\ge e^{418.72}.
\]
The same calculation excludes ambient dimensions through \(252\) for
float32 or bfloat16 and through \(934\) for float16.  Every claim in this
paragraph uses exact-real evaluation with the stated factor envelope.

For scale, deployed decoder contexts run from \(1.3\times10^{5}\) to beyond
\(10^{6}\) positions \cite{llama3herd,gemini15}; every admitted length in
the table lies below \(4.2\times10^{3}\), and the anchor length \(n=8192\)
is a sixteenth of the shorter figure.

\section{Related work}

Dimension bottlenecks give the nearest architectural statements.  Projection
dimension at least the sequence length realizes an arbitrary positive
column-stochastic pattern exactly, and below it some pattern fails
\cite{bhojanapalli2020}.  Rank trades against head count for a
nearest-neighbor target, exponentially in the rank deficit \(d-r\)
\cite{amsel2024}.  Patterns with at most \(k\) nonzero entries per row and
column admit a ratio-preserving approximation at query/key dimension
logarithmic in length, with a \(k^2\) factor \cite{likhosherstov2023}.  That
guarantee constrains only ratios among entries the target leaves nonzero.
Each charges a dimension; we solve for the magnitude a fixed dimension then
requires, at \(k=\Theta(n)\), for one named pattern.

One-layer lower bounds differ from ours in currency.  Sparse averaging over
input-specified positions charges embedding dimension \(m\) against bit
precision \(p\), with \(mp=\Omega(q)\), by set disjointness
\cite{sanford2023}.  Computing the frequency of the most frequent element charges
\(dhp=\Omega(\min\{m,n\})\) for dictionary size \(m\)
\cite{yehudai2024count}.  Its counting construction sets the query matrix to
zero---the score matrix is then input-independent---so that head is uniform
over the whole context, with centered rank \(0\) and radius \(0\) here.
Global averaging is free; the moving prefix boundary carries the entire
price.  Infinite precision removes bit counting, leaving embedding dimension
and output-MLP size \cite{kozachinskiy2024}.  We charge logit magnitude under
exact reals instead.  Bounded entries also govern the computational side:
subquadratic attention algorithms exist exactly when query/key entries stay
\(o(\sqrt{\log n})\) \cite{alman2023}; theirs bounds the time to compute a
realized matrix, ours the magnitude required to realize one.

Bounded logit spread forces global softmax attention to disperse as sequence
length grows \cite{velickovic2025}.  The same work reports average spreads
of \(5.69\) and \(5.82\) for Gemma 2B and 7B, with observed maxima \(14.78\)
and \(32.74\); no rank enters.  Lee studies row-centered logits---for an
unmasked head, our \(SP_0\)---under the name energy field and reports
concentration of their empirical singular spectrum \cite{lee2026}.  Effective
numerical rank requires a perturbation analysis before it can replace the
exact rank in our theorem.

The one-sided determinant proof is related to Alon's two-sided
perturbed-identity rank theorem \cite{alon2009}, and to the
\(\varepsilon\)-rank line that charges rank alone, with no magnitude budget
\cite{alon2013approx}.  The unnormalized prefix-sum matrix underlying \(T\)
is factorized in the differentially private continual-observation line at a
factor-norm cost logarithmic in the horizon, additively and without a rank
constraint \cite{fichtenberger2023}.

Construction-oriented treatments of transformers list prefix averaging as a
basic ingredient, \((1/i)\sum_{k\le i}x_k\), obtained from the causal mask
\cite{yang2025cookbook}.  The formal-transducer line uses masked
average-hard attention for the same prefix aggregation \cite{strobl2025}.
Neither charges a score magnitude for it.  Within the fixed-matrix exact-real
model of this paper, the law supplies the price of that ingredient once the
mask is removed: at fixed centered rank \(r\) and worst-row error \(n^{-p}\),
the centered logit radius must reach \(\exp(\Theta(n\log n/r))\), so a
position-indexed score matrix that replaces an architectural support boundary
pays a cost the masked realization does not.  The statement concerns one
fixed score matrix at one length, evaluated uniformly over bounded value
streams, and makes no claim about trained content-adaptive queries and keys,
whose scores vary with the input.

\vspace{.65ex}
\section{Scope}\label{sec:scope}

The theorem concerns a fixed attention matrix that stays constant across
bounded value probes.  Taking \(V=I_n\) reveals the matrix in one pass, while
scalar probes give an equivalent test.  Content-adaptive queries and keys need
probe channels decoupled from their computation, for example value channels
that the query and key projections ignore.  The model grants free
position-indexed tables \(Q\) and \(K\) at each length.  A shared positional
generator and downstream correction fall beyond this matrix-level claim.

The rank charge covers the pure dot-product logits.  An additive positional
bias belongs in \(S\) when it is part of that score matrix.  A separate
T5-style bias can carry the full-rank step pattern from
Proposition~\ref{prop:fullrank}; its capacity falls beyond the \(d_h\) charge.
An ALiBi-style decay also has its own positional channel, whose varying prefix
scores alone fail to give uniform prefix weights.

\section{Output error and score geometry}\label{app:profile}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:profile}]
For \(j\le i\), \(T(i,j)=1/i\), so entrywise error at most \(\varepsilon\)
gives
\[
 \frac{1-i\varepsilon}{i}\le A(i,j)\le
 \frac{1+i\varepsilon}{i}.
\]
Score differences are logarithms of probability ratios, so the oscillation on
every subset of prefix columns is at most \(\delta_i\).  For the adjacent key,
\(T(i,i+1)=0\) gives \(A(i,i+1)\le\varepsilon\), while
\(A(i,i)\ge(1-i\varepsilon)/i\), which yields
\[
 S(i,i+1)-S(i,i)
 =\log\frac{A(i,i+1)}{A(i,i)}
 \le-\log\frac{1-i\varepsilon}{i\varepsilon}.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Determinant proof}\label{app:determinant}

\begin{lemma}[Permitted permutation count]\label{lem:count}
For \(1\le k\le m\),
\[
 \#\bigl\{(\pi,K):K\subseteq[m],\ |K|=k,\ \pi\in\mathfrak S_m,\
 \ \pi(j)>j\text{ for all }j\notin K\bigr\}
 =k!\Stirling{m}{k}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(K\) and write \([m]\setminus K=\{l_1<\cdots<l_{m-k}\}\).
Assign images to \(l_{m-k},\ldots,l_1\) in that order.  When \(l_i\) is
reached, its admissible values \(\{l_i+1,\ldots,m\}\) number \(m-l_i\).
When \(i<m-k\), all \(m-k-i\) values already assigned exceed
\(l_{i+1}>l_i\); at \(i=m-k\), no values have yet been assigned.  Exactly
\(k+i-l_i\) choices remain.  Since \(l_i\ge i\) and \(l_i\le k+i\), every
factor lies in \(\{0,\ldots,k\}\).  The \(k\) unused values go to \(K\) in
\(k!\) ways, so the count for fixed \(K\) is
\[
 k!\prod_{i=1}^{m-k}(k+i-l_i).
\]

Put \(a_i=l_i-i\), the number of elements of \(K\) below \(l_i\).  Then
\(K\mapsto(a_1\le\cdots\le a_{m-k})\) is a bijection onto weakly increasing
sequences in \(\{0,\ldots,k\}\).  Reversing the order and setting
\(i_j=k-a_{m-k+1-j}\) gives
\[
 \sum_K\prod_{i=1}^{m-k}(k+i-l_i)
 =\sum_{0\le i_1\le\cdots\le i_{m-k}\le k}i_1\cdots i_{m-k}
 =\sum_{1\le i_1\le\cdots\le i_{m-k}\le k}i_1\cdots i_{m-k}
 =h_{m-k}(1,2,\ldots,k)
 =\Stirling{m}{k}.
\]
Terms containing a zero factor vanish, which gives the second equality.  The
last identity follows from
\[
 h_d(1,\ldots,k)=h_d(1,\ldots,k-1)+k\,h_{d-1}(1,\ldots,k).
\]
With \(d=m-k\), this is the Stirling recurrence
\(\Stirling{m}{k}=\Stirling{m-1}{k-1}+k\Stirling{m-1}{k}\).
The initial values \(h_0(1,\ldots,k)=1\) and
\(h_d(1)=1\) match \(\Stirling{k}{k}=1\) and
\(\Stirling{m}{1}=1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:subset}]
Take the principal submatrix \(D_I=D[I,I]\).  Its rank is at most \(r\).
Let \(U_I\) be its upper-triangular part and \(E_I=D_I-U_I\).  Then
\[
 |\det U_I|\ge\prod_{i\in I}\gamma_i,\qquad U_I=D_I-E_I.
\]
Expand the determinant multilinearly in columns.  Index a term by
\(K\subseteq I\), the columns drawn from \(D_I\); the remaining columns come
from \(E_I\).  Terms with \(|K|>r\) vanish.  The last column of \(E_I\) is
zero, so every surviving term has \(|K|\ge1\).

Fix \(|K|=k\).  Order \(I\) increasingly and identify it with \([m]\).
In the Leibniz expansion, a product can be nonzero when
\(\pi(j)>j\) for every \(j\notin K\), because column \(j\) of \(E_I\) is
supported strictly below row \(j\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:count} counts the pairs
\((\pi,K)\) with this property.

Every product uses \(k\) entries bounded by \(2B\).  The other \(m-k\)
entries have distinct row indices and contribute at most
\[
 \max_{\substack{R\subseteq I\\|R|=k}}
 \prod_{i\in I\setminus R}\delta_i .
\]
Summing absolute values proves \eqref{eq:weighted}; replacing all
\(\delta_i,\gamma_i\) by their common bounds proves
\eqref{eq:constant}.
\end{proof}

\section{Hadamard companion and all-rank floor}
\label{app:hadamard}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hadamard}
Under the constant-profile hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:subset}, with \(m=|I|\),
\[
 \gamma^m\le
 \sum_{k=1}^{\min\{r,m\}}
 e_{m-k}(1,\sqrt2,\ldots,\sqrt{m-1})\,
 \delta^{m-k}(2B\sqrt m)^k ,
\]
where \(e_j\) is the elementary symmetric polynomial.  Also
\[
 B\ge\gamma/2.
\]
More generally, for row-dependent gaps in Theorem~\ref{thm:subset},
\[
 B\ge\frac12\max_{i\in I}\gamma_i.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use the same column expansion.  A column \(j\) drawn from \(E_I\) has norm at
most \(\delta\sqrt{m-j}\), and a column drawn from \(D_I\) has norm at most
\(2B\sqrt m\).  Hadamard's inequality and summation over the selected columns
give the displayed elementary symmetric polynomial.  Profiled row \(i\)
contains two entries separated by at least \(\gamma_i\); after centering, one
has magnitude at least \(\gamma_i/2\).  Maximizing over the profiled rows gives
the general floor, including its constant-profile specialization.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:budget}]
Take \(I=[N]\) and use
\[
 e_{N-k}(1,\sqrt2,\ldots,\sqrt{N-1})
 \le\binom{N-1}{k-1}(\sqrt N)^{N-k}.
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:hadamard} gives \(2B\ge\gamma\); in the present output
profile, \(\gamma\ge\delta\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  For every
\(k\le r\),
\[
 (\delta\sqrt N)^{N-k}(2B\sqrt N)^k
 \le(\delta\sqrt N)^{N-r}(2B\sqrt N)^r.
\]
Summing the binomial coefficients yields
\[
 \gamma^N\le
 2^{N-1}(\delta\sqrt N)^{N-r}(2B\sqrt N)^r,
\]
and rearrangement gives
\[
 N\log\frac{\gamma}{\delta\sqrt N}
 \le(N-1)\log2+r\log\frac{2B}{\delta}.
\]
At \(\varepsilon=n^{-p}\), Lemma~\ref{lem:profile} gives
\(\gamma=(p-1)\log n\,(1+o(1))\) and
\(\delta=2n^{1-p}(1+o(1))\), so the left side is
\(N((p-3/2)\log n+O(\log\log n))\).  With \(B\le n^c\),
\(\log(2B/\delta)\le(c+p-1)\log n+O(1)\).  Dividing by
\(N\log n\) proves the corollary.
\end{proof}

\section{The upper construction}\label{app:construction}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:upper}]
For \(1\le i\le N\), set
\[
 \delta_i=\frac{i\varepsilon}{16},\qquad
 \gamma_i=\log\frac{8(n-i)}{i\varepsilon},\qquad
 w_i=\log\left(1+\frac{\gamma_i}{\delta_i}\right).
\]
When \(0<\varepsilon\le1/n\), their margins satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:margins}
 \delta_i=\frac{i\varepsilon}{16}\le\frac1{16},
 \qquad
 \gamma_i=\log\frac{8(n-i)}{i\varepsilon}\ge\log 8,
 \qquad
 \frac{\gamma_i}{\delta_i}\ge16\log 8>33,
\end{equation}
using \(i\varepsilon\le N/n<1\) and \(n-i\ge1\).  These inequalities give
\(e^{\delta_i}\le e^{1/16}<2\) and \(\gamma_i>\delta_i\).

Partition \([N]\) into exactly \(r\) consecutive
nonempty blocks so that, for every \(I=\{a,\ldots,b\}\),
\[
 \sum_{t=a+1}^{b}w_t\le W/r,\qquad W=\sum_{i=1}^Nw_i.
\]
A greedy partition creates at most \(r\) blocks: at each cut, the completed
internal load plus the skipped boundary weight exceeds \(W/r\), and these
charged sets are disjoint.  If fewer blocks appear, \(r\le N\) guarantees a
block with at least two rows.  Splitting such blocks reaches exactly \(r\)
and removes internal transitions.

For one block, define
\[
 c_a=1,\qquad G_t=\sum_{s=a}^{t}\gamma_sc_s,\qquad
 c_t=\frac{G_{t-1}}{\delta_t}\quad(t>a),
\]
\[
 u_i^I=\frac{\mathbf1[i\in I]}{c_i},\qquad
 v_j^I=-\sum_{t=a}^{\min\{b,j-1\}}\gamma_tc_t,
\qquad
 S=\sum_Iu^I(v^I)^\top .
\]
The \(u^I\) have disjoint nonempty supports.  The first differences of the
\(v^I\) have disjoint nonempty supports as well, so \(\rank(S)=r\).  Suppose
\(\sum_I a_IP_0v^I=0\).  Then \(\sum_Ia_Iv^I\) is constant.  Taking first
differences and using their disjoint nonzero supports gives \(a_I=0\) for
every block, so \(\rho(S)=r\).

By \eqref{eq:margins}, \(G_{t-1}\ge\gamma_ac_a=\gamma_a\ge\log8\) and
\(\delta_t\le1/16\), so every \(c_t\ge1\).

For \(i\in I=[a,b]\), the row has the explicit form
\[
 S(i,j)=
 \begin{cases}
  0,&j\le a,\\
  -G_{j-1}/c_i,&a<j\le b+1,\\
  -G_b/c_i,&j>b+1.
 \end{cases}
\]
Its prefix scores have maximum zero and range at most \(\delta_i\).  The
score drops by \(\gamma_i\) after key \(i\).  Let \(\one_i\) denote the
length-\(i\) all-ones vector, and let
\(z_j=-S(i,j)\in[0,\delta_i]\) for \(j\le i\).  By
\eqref{eq:margins},
\[
 G_t/G_{t-1}=1+\gamma_t/\delta_t\ge2,
\]
so geometric summation gives \(\sum_{j\le i}z_j\le2\delta_i\).  Put
\(a_j=e^{-z_j}\), \(Z=\sum_{j\le i}a_j\), and let \(\widehat q\) denote the
attention distribution conditioned on the prefix.  Since
\(Z\ge i e^{-\delta_i}\) and \(1-e^{-z_j}\le z_j\),
\[
 \sum_{j\le i}\left|\frac{a_j}{Z}-\frac1i\right|
 \le \frac{2}{Z}\sum_{j\le i}(1-a_j)
 \le \frac{2e^{\delta_i}}i\sum_{j\le i}z_j.
\]
Using \(e^{\delta_i}<2\) from \eqref{eq:margins} now gives
\[
 \left\|\widehat q-\frac{\one_i}{i}\right\|_1
 \le\frac{2e^{\delta_i}}i\sum_{j\le i}z_j
 \le\frac{4e^{\delta_i}\delta_i}i
 =\frac{e^{\delta_i}\varepsilon}4
 \le\varepsilon/2.
\]
Every future score is at most \(-z_i-\gamma_i\), and each prefix score is at
least \(-z_i\).  The future-to-prefix mass ratio bounds the future probability
\(\lambda_i\), giving
\[
 \lambda_i\le\frac{n-i}{i}e^{-\gamma_i}=\varepsilon/8.
\]
On the prefix, \(A(i,:)= (1-\lambda_i)\widehat q\).  The triangle inequality
gives full row error at most
\(\varepsilon/2+2\lambda_i\le3\varepsilon/4\).  Row \(n\) is exact.

For a block,
\[
 G_b=\gamma_a\prod_{t=a+1}^{b}
 \left(1+\frac{\gamma_t}{\delta_t}\right),
\]
so \(\log G_b\le\log L_\varepsilon+W/r\), where
\(L_\varepsilon=\log(8n/\varepsilon)\).  Since
\(\gamma_t\le L_\varepsilon\), \(\gamma_t/\delta_t>1\), and
\(N!\ge(N/e)^N\),
\[
 w_t\le\log\frac{32L_\varepsilon}{t\varepsilon},
\]
\[
 W\le N\log\frac{32L_\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}-\log N!
 \le N\log\frac{32eL_\varepsilon}{N\varepsilon}
 \le N\log\frac{64eL_\varepsilon}{n\varepsilon}.
\]
Since every \(c_i\ge1\), row \(i\) of \(S\) has entries \(v^I_j/c_i\) of
magnitude at most \(G_b\).  Its range bounds every centered entry, which gives
\eqref{eq:upperbudget}.
\end{proof}
